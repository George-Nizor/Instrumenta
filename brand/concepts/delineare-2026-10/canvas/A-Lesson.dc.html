The idea

A cube has flat planes, so each face takes one flat tone. A sphere is curved everywhere, so it takes a gradient. A cylinder and a cone have both: flat along their length, curved around it. Most objects you will ever draw, real or invented, break down into these five, so once one light behaves consistently across all of them, everything else follows.

Common mistakes

  • A gradient on the cube's flat faces
  • The same gradient on every form, whatever its shape
  • No contact shadow, so objects hover
  • The lightest area at the edge, which reverses the light
