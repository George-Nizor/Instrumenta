Instrumenta brand directions

Three complete directions for the icon family, typeface and colour system, with the same eight concepts drawn in each so you are comparing treatments rather than drawings. Hover an icon to see its motion, or press one to see it as the app's header. The glyphs are rough first drafts; the choice here is the system.

Today

Rendered, photoreal sculptures. They look impressive at 1024 px, turn to mud at 24 px in a taskbar, can't be animated without regenerating them, and the materials are inconsistent (a plastic rook beside glass and paper). Most of them don't say what the app does.

Colour, shared by every direction

One lightness and chroma for every accent, in OKLCH, so no app looks louder than another; only the hue changes. Hues are spread around the wheel. Forge3D moves from orange to ember, so it no longer competes with Instrumenta's brass, and Luna moves to a pale lunar silver-blue that suits a monochrome-first app.

What to decide

  1. Which direction, or which parts of which (A's tile with B's type is a valid answer).
  2. Tile or no tile. A shared container is what Windows, macOS and app stores expect, and it makes the eight read as one family in a taskbar. The current brand rule forbids it; this would reverse that rule on purpose.
  3. Instrumenta's glyph. A carry case reads as "the box your tools come in." A tuning fork or a stylised "i" are the alternatives.
  4. Luna. Its current 1-bit look survives fully only in direction C. With A or B it gets archived under brand/archive/v1/ and a tagged release.
