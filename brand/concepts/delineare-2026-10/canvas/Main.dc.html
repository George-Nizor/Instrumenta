Delineare

dē·lī·ne·ā·re to sketch out, to draw the outline of

Ludere is to play, Discere is to learn, Delineare is to draw. The verb is rare in classical Latin, and its one use is about drawing: Apelles, tricked into a dinner at Ptolemy's court, picks up a dead coal and draws a face on the wall.

Arrepto carbone extincto e foculo imaginem in pariete delineavit.

He snatched a dead coal from the brazier and drew a likeness on the wall.

Pliny the Elder, Natural History 35.89
Possible motto

Nulla dies sine linea. No day without a line. The proverb comes from Pliny 35.84 on Apelles, by way of Erasmus.
