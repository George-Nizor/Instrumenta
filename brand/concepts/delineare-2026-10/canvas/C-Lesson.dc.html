Chapter 41

Shading basic forms

The sphere, the cube, the cylinder, the cone and the egg, and stacks of them.

TIER
Essential
TIME
6 to 12 h, estimated
FREE
Partial coverage
READY
Prerequisites done

A cube has flat planes, so each face takes one flat tone. A sphere is curved everywhere, so it takes a gradient. A cylinder and a cone have both: flat along their length, curved around it.

Most objects you will ever draw, real or invented, break down into these five. Once one light behaves the same way across all of them, everything else follows. Watch the sphere demonstration first1, then put the lesson away and draw.

The lightest spot is never at the edge. If it is, the light has been reversed.

Every form needs a dark contact patch where it meets the table, or it floats.

Exercises

  1. 1Five forms, one light. All five on one page, lit from the upper left, the same four tones on each. One page, 60 min.
  2. 2Smooth pass. The same five, blended into smooth transitions. One page, 60 min.
  3. 3Stack and overlap. Predict where each cast shadow falls before you shade, then check against a photo. Three drawings, 90 min.
  4. 4Primitives from the kitchen. A mug, a ball, a box, a funnel and an egg under a desk lamp. Five objects, 100 min.

For exercise 3, photograph a real stack of boxes, tins and a ball to check your prediction.

Watch for the same gradient on every form, whatever its shape.
