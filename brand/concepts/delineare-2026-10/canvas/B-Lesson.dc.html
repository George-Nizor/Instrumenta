Light, value and form

Shading basic forms

Essential6 to 12 hours, estimatedFree coverage: partialReady: both prerequisites done

A cube has flat planes, so each face takes one flat tone. A sphere is curved everywhere, so it takes a gradient. A cylinder and a cone have both: flat along their length, curved around it. Most objects you will ever draw, real or invented, break down into these five.

By the end you can

  • Shade each form with the tone behaviour its surface needs
  • Keep one light direction across a whole page
  • Cast believable shadows between stacked forms
  • Break a real object into primitives and shade it as such

Watch for

  • A gradient on the cube's flat faces
  • The same gradient on every form, whatever its shape
  • No contact shadow, so objects hover
  • The lightest area at the edge, which reverses the light
